Every node
keeps the books.

P2Poolv2 is a bitcoin mining pool with no operator keeping books on your behalf. Every node replays every share, arrives at the same payout, and miners are paid straight out of the block’s coinbase. No account, no withdrawal, no one who can quietly drop you from the ledger.

A pool controls you through its accounting

DATUM and Stratum V2 let a miner build their own block template, and that is real progress. But both leave the share accounting on a server the pool operates. That server decides what goes in the payout distribution it hands back to you. It can stop counting your shares, and by the time anyone can see it happening, the block is already mined and the money is already gone.

P2Poolv2 moves that computation to every node. The share chain is replicated, the PPLNS window is recomputed locally, and the coinbase your miner is working on is one you can check yourself before you hash a single nonce on it.

Governments are good at cutting off the heads of centrally controlled networks like Napster, but pure P2P networks like Gnutella and Tor seem to be holding their own.

What we changed since the first P2Pool

The original P2Pool proved the idea and then ran into three walls: orphaned shares at scale, hashrate readings that made miners think it was broken, and a coinbase that could only hold so many outputs. Each has a specific answer here.

A graph, not a line

Shares reference up to three uncles, so work that arrives late or races another share still gets paid instead of being orphaned. An uncle is paid with a 10% discount, the share that references it earns a 10% bonus, and each uncle can be claimed once. More miners fit in the chain, and the pool’s variance drops as they join.

Vardiff for each miner

P2Pool handed every rig the whole pool’s difficulty, which sampled far too slowly to report a small miner’s hashrate honestly. Our Stratum v1 server, written from scratch in Rust, runs per-miner vardiff at a three second target, the same as CKPool. Your local dashboard shows your rigs; a separate dashboard shows the whole chain.

Shares that carry Script

The share chain has its own UTXOs and its own Script, built on rust-bitcoin. Every share has a share-chain coinbase paying one output to an address you control. That output is the thing you can sell, which is how payouts get past the limit on coinbase outputs that capped the original P2Pool.

Compact blocks on the wire

A share is a compact block of roughly 30 kB rather than a full block. At five shares every ten seconds, a node relays the chain on about 1.3 Mbps instead of 176 Mbps. Home connections can carry a full node.

That number is the answer to the only question a small miner really has: will I ever land a share here? Land one share in a retarget window and you have something to sell, and you can see for yourself that the pool is paying what it owes.

  • Run your own node, or use someone else’s. A private node in front of your own farm keeps your work and your payouts under your roof. A public node lets other miners point hashrate at you.
  • Bring two addresses. Your bitcoin address receives the PPLNS payout from a found block’s coinbase. Your P2Poolv2 address owns the share-chain coinbase, which is the asset you trade. Neither is derived from the other, and the share address must be spend-capable.
  • Set it from your miner’s password field. Put p2p=<your p2pool address> first in the password field so firmware that truncates long passwords still leaves a valid address. Running your own node instead? Set it once in config.toml and leave the rigs alone.
  • Watch it in Grafana. The node exposes Prometheus metrics at /metrics, with dashboards for per-rig hashrate, chain-wide hashrate by miner, effort into the current block, and coinbase distribution.

If you build payments

A bitcoin coinbase cannot pay thousands of miners, and mining firmware caps it tighter still. That limit is what stopped the original P2Pool from growing. So P2Poolv2 pays the top N miners directly from the coinbase and turns everyone else’s work into a share-chain output they can sell for bitcoin. Market makers buy those outputs and take the coinbase slot in their place.

The swap is an HTLC on the share chain against a Lightning invoice, with the same payment hash on both sides. It settles in seconds and neither side has to trust the other.

  1. Alice invoices

    The miner picks a 32-byte preimage and raises a BOLT11 or BOLT12 invoice for the sats she wants, carrying its hash.

  2. Alice locks her shares

    She locks her share-chain outputs in an HTLC under the same payment hash, so only the preimage releases them.

  3. Bob checks both sides

    The market maker confirms the share HTLC is final and that the hash in it matches the hash in the invoice.

  4. Bob pays over Lightning

    Settling the invoice reveals the preimage to him. That is the whole trick: paying is what unlocks the shares.

  5. Bob claims the shares

    He spends the HTLC on the share chain with the preimage. If he never pays, Alice refunds herself after the timeout, or they both sign an instant refund.

The trading window

A share-chain coinbase matures after about a day of share blocks, the same reason bitcoin makes you wait: it stops a miner selling shares and then reorganising them away. The window closes when the PPLNS accounting window moves past that depth, because the share no longer counts toward the next payout. Between those two edges, the output is tradable, which also makes it a very short-dated hashrate contract.

Run a node

You need a bitcoin node the P2Poolv2 node can talk to, and Rust. There is a docker signet if you would rather not point anything real at it yet. 

# build and run against your own bitcoind
$ git clone https://github.com/p2poolv2/p2poolv2
$ cd p2poolv2
$ just run

# or bring up a local signet with bitcoind and a miner
$ cd docker && docker compose build
$ ./run-p2pool.sh signet bitcoind
# point a rig at the node
url      stratum+tcp://<your-node>:3333
user     <your bitcoin address>
password p2p=<your p2pool address>

Share addresses are bech32m: p2pool1 on mainnet, tp2pool1 on testnet4, sp2pool1 on signet. Put p2p= first in the password field so a truncating firmware still leaves a valid address behind.

Where the code is today

P2Poolv2 runs on testnet4 and signet. It is not ready for your mainnet hashrate, and we would rather say so than collect it.
